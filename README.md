Day 1 — Thursday 19 November · Arrive Tokyo

No alarm.

No alarm, no plan. Whatever time you land, today is for nothing in particular.

On arrival: Haneda. Visit Japan Web QR at immigration, Suica into Apple Wallet before you leave the plane. Haneda to Kuramae is one through-train, about 35 minutes.

Afternoon: Drop bags, walk the river. Dandelion Chocolate's factory café is on the corner.

21:00 — Senso-ji lit up with nobody in it. Completely different place after nine.

Konbini: Your first 7-Eleven run. Egg sando, and the pudding.

Day 2 — Friday 20 November · Kamakura

Earlier start, around 09:00.

Friday, and the only clean weekday in the Tokyo block — which is exactly why Kamakura goes here rather than on the holiday weekend.

09:00 — JR Yokosuka line to Kita-Kamakura, about an hour.

10:15 — Engaku-ji and Meigetsu-in off the platform, then the wooded path south into Kamakura proper.

11:45 — Tsurugaoka Hachimangu, up the wide approach through its torii.

12:45 — Komachi-dori for lunch on your feet — shirasu rice bowls, croquettes, purple sweet potato soft serve.

14:00 — Hokoku-ji: a small bamboo grove where you sit at the back with a bowl of matcha. Quieter than anything in Arashiyama, which is part of why you can skip Arashiyama.

15:15 — Enoden two stops to Hase. Hasedera, and the Great Buddha ten minutes away. The Hasedera illumination may only just be starting this early in the season.

16:20 — Enoden to Kamakura-Kohōmae. Sunset over the sea, and on a clear evening Fuji sits on the horizon across the bay.

19:30 — Back in Tokyo. Nothing planned.

Day 3 — Saturday 21 November · Cameras, then clothes

Late start.

Saturday, and the first day of a three-day national holiday. Tokyo handles it better than anywhere — it is where everyone leaves from.

10:30 — Map Camera, Shinjuku, at opening. Five floors, the deepest used inventory in Japan, graded honestly, tax-free with your passport. Used floors first.

11:45 — Kitamura, Alps Camera, Lemon-sha and Chuko Camera Box, all within the same blocks. Prices differ on identical glass, so this is where you compare.

13:30 — Nakano: Fujiya Camera and its used annex, then Nakano Broadway. Older manual glass is priced better here.

15:30 — Clothes. Aoyama and Ura-Harajuku: PUBLIC TOKYO for urban minimal, then Graphpaper, Auralee, Comoli, Lemaire, stein, Nanamica.

17:30 — Second-hand designer at Ragtag and Kindal, Harajuku. This is where the value is.

19:30 — Kikanbo in Kanda, spicy miso. Pork and chicken base.

21:30 — Omoide Yokocho, then Golden Gai. Albatross and Bar Araku take walk-ins and are used to foreigners. Cover ¥500–1,500; ask before you sit. Last trains around 00:30.

Day 4 — Sunday 22 November · Flea market, Yanaka, tea, Shibuya at sunset

Late start.

09:30 — Sunday. Tomioka Hachimangu's antique flea market in Monzen-nakacho, fifteen minutes from Kuramae. Small, local, and the only market on this trip whose date actually lines up.

11:30 — Yanaka. Wooden houses, cats, the cemetery avenue in late colour. Yanaka Ginza for a menchi-katsu on the street.

13:30 — Tea, the non-viral kind: Tokyo Saryo in Sangenjaya, single-origin sencha hand-dripped at a nine-seat counter. Reserve well ahead — it is a Sunday.

16:00 — Shibuya Sky for sunset, around 16:25 at this point in November. Holiday-weekend sunset slots vanish the day they release, so book about a month out.

17:30 — If it sold out: Tokyu Plaza 17F rooftop, Sakura Stage terrace, Hikarie 11F, or the crossing from the Starbucks in Q-Front. All free, all good.

19:30 — Nonbei Yokocho, behind Shibuya station. Six-seat bars where you cannot avoid talking to whoever is next to you.

Day 5 — Monday 23 November · Slow west Tokyo

Late start.

Holiday Monday. Deliberately light, and deliberately not a day trip — everywhere outside the city is full today.

11:00 — Nakameguro and Daikanyama: 1LDK, Okura for indigo, Nanamica. I'm donut? before noon or the good flavours are gone.

14:00 — Shimokitazawa for vintage: New York Joe Exchange, Flamingo, Toyo Hyakkaten. Or Koenji, scruffier and cheaper.

16:00 — Meiji Jingu Gaien ginkgo avenue. Two weeks early for full gold, so expect green tipping to yellow rather than the postcard. Meiji Shrine through the forest afterwards.

19:00 — Ebisu Yokocho: communal tables, loud, the fastest way in Tokyo to end up drinking with strangers.

23:00 — The SG Club in Shibuya if you want one proper cocktail bar. Sleep in tomorrow; you are only moving as far as a mountain.

Day 6 — Tuesday 24 November · Tokyo to Kawaguchiko

Late start.

Tuesday. The holiday crowds went home last night, which is the single best thing about these dates.

10:00 — Check out. Fuji Excursion from Shinjuku, two hours, reserved.

12:00 — Drop bags. Walk the town and scout the Lawson angle in daylight so you know exactly where to stand tomorrow at dawn.

15:00 — Chureito Pagoda — 400 steps up from Shimoyoshida. On a Tuesday in late November you may have the platform close to yourself.

16:20 — Honcho street in Fujiyoshida on the way down: Fuji framed at the end of a sloping shopping street. Also your fallback if the Lawson view is screened.

17:00 — The lake maples are at their tail end around now — the Momiji Corridor on the north shore is worth ten minutes if you pass it.

18:30 — The Lawson after dark. Sign lit, mountain behind, nobody there. The better version of that photograph.

20:00 — Dinner, bath, early night. This is the one you set an alarm for.

Day 7 — Wednesday 25 November · Fuji, all day

The early one.

06:10 — The Lawson at first light. The one genuinely early morning of the trip, and the reason you came. Fuji grows its own cap cloud as the ground warms, so this hour decides everything. Stand on the legal side; there are wardens.

07:30 — North shore at Ubuyagasaki or Oishi Park for the inverted reflection — the water is stillest just after sunrise.

09:30 — Oshino Hakkai at opening, before the coaches. Eight spring-fed ponds with the mountain behind.

12:00 — Lake Yamanaka and the Panorama-dai viewpoint. Long lunch.

14:30 — Mt Tenjo by the Kachi Kachi ropeway, then walk down through the woods.

17:00 — Onsen. Yurari or Benifuji-no-Yu give you Fuji from the outdoor bath for about ¥1,000 — which is how you get the ryokan feeling inside your budget.

Day 8 — Thursday 26 November · Optional second dawn, then Kyoto

Alarm optional.

06:10 — Only if yesterday clouded over. Insurance, not obligation — if the shot is in the bag, sleep until nine.

11:00 — Bus Kawaguchiko to Mishima, 1 h 45.

Optional: Fuji Dream Bridge detour: Kodama from Mishima to Shizuoka is 25 min, then a taxi each way. Two hours door to door, and only on a clear morning.

13:40 — Shinkansen to Kyoto. Right-hand side, seats D or E — Fuji fills the window for the first ten minutes out of Mishima.

16:30 — Check in. Nothing demanding today.

19:00 — Nishiki Market as it closes, then Pontocho alley for dinner.

Day 9 — Friday 27 November · The Eizan line into the northern mountains

Late start. First in the Kyoto block on purpose — northern Kyoto colours a week or two ahead of the city, so this day is best taken early and on a weekday.

10:30 — Demachiyanagi and the Eizan railway.

10:50 — take the Kirara observation car if one comes, with its side-facing windows. Between Ichihara and Ninose the line runs through the momiji tunnel, where the train slows right down inside a canopy of maples.

11:30 — Kurama. The temple climb through exposed cedar roots to the summit, then over the ridge and down into Kibune — about an hour of walking, and the best hour of the Kyoto week.

14:00 — Kifune Shrine and its stone lantern staircase. Lunch in Kibune.

15:30 — Eizan back. If Ruriko-in's autumn opening is running, change for the Yase branch — these dates give it a real chance where the December ones did not. Timed tickets, book ahead.

17:00 — otherwise stop at Ichijōji for Shisen-dō and Enkō-ji, two small quiet temples with very good late colour.

19:00 — Ichijōji ramen street. Menya Gokkei for thick chicken paitan.

Day 10 — Saturday 28 November · Uji all day, Fushimi Inari at night

Late start. Saturday in peak season — and today is built to sidestep it. Uji absorbs a weekend far better than central Kyoto, and Inari after dark has no crowd at all.

10:30 — JR south to Uji, twenty-five minutes from Kyoto station.

11:15 — Byōdō-in and the Phoenix Hall on its pond; the interior needs a separate timed ticket. Then Byōdō-in Omotesandō for lunch — matcha soba, matcha everything.

14:00 — matcha culture, hands on. Grind your own tea in a stone mill at Fukujuen's Ujicha Kōbō or the Chazuna tea museum. Then compare houses: Nakamura Tōkichi for the famous parfait, Marukyu Koyamaen and Itoh Kyuemon for the serious leaf.

16:00 — Taihō-an, the municipal tea house. A formal ceremony for about ¥500, unhurried, nearly always empty. The one you'll actually remember.

17:15 — the Uji river at dusk, then the JR Nara line back. Inari station is twelve minutes up.

18:45 — Fushimi Inari at night. The shrine never closes and never charges, and after dark the senbon torii glow from within while the crowd has gone home. Walk to Yotsutsuji for the city below and turn back there. Take a torch — above that it's genuinely dark, and the wild boar signs aren't decorative.

21:30 — ramen on the way home.

Day 11 — Sunday 29 November · Kinkaku-ji, Nijō, and Kiyomizu after dark

Late start. Sunday in peak koyo, so today deliberately favours big sites that swallow a crowd over narrow ones that choke on it.

10:30 — Kinkaku-ji. Twenty minutes is enough; it's one view on a one-way loop, which keeps moving even when busy.

11:30 — Ryōan-ji's rock garden, fifteen minutes away, then Ninna-ji if you're enjoying yourself.

14:00 — Nijō Castle. Ninomaru Palace and the nightingale floors, which squeak deliberately underfoot. A big site on a big plot, so a Sunday barely registers. Last entry around 16:00.

16:30 — bus east. Coffee somewhere near Gion before the light goes.

18:00 — Kiyomizu-dera's autumn night opening. Lit hillside, the beam of light thrown into the sky, the whole veranda after dark. Verify the dates; these ones sit comfortably inside the usual window.

21:00 — Pontocho or Kiyamachi. You're twelve minutes' walk from your bed.

Day 12 — Monday 30 November · Tōfuku-ji, Higashiyama, Eikan-dō lit

Late start. Last day in Kyoto.

10:00 — Tōfuku-ji on a Monday. The Tsūtenkyō bridge over a valley of maples, exactly in its week and without the weekend crush — which is precisely why it sits here and not yesterday.

12:00 — north to Kōdai-ji and its bamboo path, then Yasaka Shrine and Maruyama Park.

14:00 — matcha, properly. Ippodo's Kaboku tearoom on Teramachi, brewed in front of you with the water temperature explained. Founded 1717 and still not a queue attraction.

15:45 — Gion. Hanamikōji, then Shirakawa lane along the canal, which is the prettier half and takes a fraction of the traffic.

17:00 — the Philosopher's Path at dusk and the Nanzen-ji aqueduct. Both outdoors and open when the halls have shut.

18:00 — Eikan-dō's autumn illumination. The hillside lit and reflected in the pond — the best night photograph in Kyoto, and a good last night here.

20:30 — late dinner on Kiyamachi.

Day 13 — Tuesday 1 December · Slow morning, then Osaka

Late start. Nothing booked before eleven.

11:00 — a last coffee, or anything you missed. Kyoto station is fifteen minutes away whenever you decide to move.

13:00 — train to Osaka. Thirty minutes.

14:30 — check in at Nipponbashi.

15:00 — Den Den Town, directly below you. Second-hand electronics and camera gear, sometimes under Tokyo prices on older lenses. Kuromon Market next door.

18:00 — Shinsekai and Tsūtenkaku, far better after dark. Kushikatsu in Janjan Yokocho — one dip in the communal sauce, no second dip, and they will tell you.

20:30 — Dotonbori for the neon, briefly, then the smaller streets behind it.

Day 14 — Wednesday 2 December · Himeji

09:30 start. A Wednesday, which matters here — the castle is a different place midweek.

09:30 — JR Special Rapid from Osaka, about an hour, ¥1,520 each way and no Shinkansen surcharge.

10:45 — out of the station and straight down the avenue. The castle sits at the end of it, framed, a fifteen-minute walk away. That approach is the photograph.

11:00 — Himeji Castle. The only great Japanese castle never burned, bombed or rebuilt in concrete — everything you climb through is original timber. The keep is six storeys of steep wooden stairs, so wear soft shoes you can take off easily.

13:00 — Kōko-en next door: nine walled Edo gardens, very good late colour, and a tea house where you sit with matcha looking at the moat.

14:30 — Mount Shosha. Bus, then the ropeway up to Engyō-ji — wooden halls strung along a cedar ridge above the city, where The Last Samurai was filmed. Quiet, atmospheric, and the best of the day if the weather holds. Skip it if it's raining and go back for a second look at the castle instead.

18:45 — back in Osaka. Dinner in Namba.

Day 15 — Thursday 3 December · Nara

09:30 start — ahead of the coaches, and on a weekday.

10:30 — Tōdai-ji and the Great Buddha, the largest wooden building you'll ever stand inside.

12:00 — Kasuga Taisha's stone lantern paths through the forest.

13:30 — Nigatsu-dō's terrace. Free, open, and hardly anyone climbs to it. The whole valley from above.

15:00 — Nara Park holds its colour into December and the deer are genuinely everywhere. The most relaxed photography of the trip.

18:00 — back to Osaka. Okonomiyaki, karaoke in Namba.

Day 16 — Friday 4 December · Minoh Falls, then the last night

Late start.

10:30 — Hankyu from Umeda to Minoo, about thirty minutes. Out of the city and into a forested gorge in under an hour — on a Friday rather than a Sunday, which matters here.

11:15 — the walk up the valley is 2.7 km on a paved riverside path, roughly 45 minutes each way, uphill but gentle. The first week of December is exactly when this gorge peaks.

12:15 — Minoh Falls, 33 metres straight down into the pool.

13:00 — momiji tempura on the way back: maple leaves salted, battered and fried, sold from stalls along the path. A local thing, not a tourist thing.

15:00 — back in Namba. Desserts: Rikuro Ojisan cheesecake eaten warm; Gram Café pancakes if you want the queue.

18:30 — Jinrui Mina Menrui in Esaka, or Menya Joroku.

20:30 — Ura-Namba on a Friday night, which is the best it gets. Standing bars and people who start conversations unprompted — your last and best night for making friends.

Day 17 — Saturday 5 December · Fly home

11:00 — Nankai Rapi:t or JR Haruka from Namba to Kansai, 40–75 minutes. This assumes an afternoon departure; a 09:00 flight would mean leaving Namba at six.

Three hours early — tax-free purchases can be inspected. Keep receipts sealed until past Japanese customs.

Land in Bengaluru late on Saturday night, with Sunday to recover.

Stays

Four beds in sixteen nights. Indicative solo rates, all inside your ₹5,000 ceiling.

Tokyo, 19–24 Nov — Nui. Hostel & Bar Lounge, Kuramae. The ground-floor bar is the easiest place in Tokyo to fall into conversation. Dorm ¥3,500–5,000; private ¥9,000–13,000.

Kawaguchiko, 24–26 Nov — K's House Mt Fuji or Kagelow, dorm ¥3,000–5,000. The ryokan doesn't fit your rule: a Fuji-view ryokan for one with meals runs ¥20,000–90,000, three to ten times over, and many refuse single occupancy. Buy the experience separately — a day-use onsen at Yurari or Benifuji-no-Yu gives you the mountain from an outdoor bath for about ¥1,000.

Kyoto, 26 Nov – 1 Dec — Len Kyoto Kawaramachi (bar downstairs, sociable) or Piece Hostel Sanjo. Dorm ¥3,500–6,000. Peak week with a weekend inside it. Book this before anything else.

Osaka, 1–5 Dec — Hostel Mitsuwaya in Nipponbashi, dorm ¥3,000–5,000, guest events most evenings. Alternatives: J-Hoppers, Hostel 64, First Cabin Namba, Hotel Chuo Selene.

Where the transport money goes

Two legs dominate: Mishima to Kyoto is ¥10,540 and the Fuji Excursion is ¥4,130. Himeji is cheap because the JR Special Rapid needs no Shinkansen surcharge. Neither the nationwide JR Pass nor the Kansai–Hiroshima pass pays for itself, so buy individual tickets and keep Suica or ICOCA topped up.

Free, and among the best of it

Fushimi Inari at night, Chureito Pagoda, the Lawson, Oshino Hakkai, Meiji Shrine and the Gaien ginkgo, Yanaka, the Tomioka Hachimangū flea market, Tsurugaoka Hachimangū, the Nanzen-ji aqueduct, the Philosopher's Path, Kifune Shrine, Nigatsu-dō's terrace, Minoh Falls, and every yokochō you drink in.

Shopping and customs

Kept separate, but keep the number in view: the Indian duty-free allowance is ₹50,000 of goods on arrival, and roughly 38% duty applies above it. If you carry expensive gear out of India, file an export certificate at Bengaluru before departure. The budget tab tracks the remaining allowance as you enter what you've spent.

Before you book anything
Kyoto accommodation first. Peak maple week sells out months ahead at roughly double the normal rate. Before the flights, if you have to.
Get the visa file moving. Everything else is refundable; the visa is the long pole.
Hold refundable beds for the Fuji nights and the Namba weekend.
Reserve the Fuji Excursion seat when booking opens, about a month out.
Book Shibuya Sky's sunset slot the day tickets are released.
Reserve Tokyo Saryo, and Sakurai if you want it.
Watch for the Ruriko-in autumn dates, and the Lawson screen status.
Watch the Kyoto foliage forecast in early November. If the season runs early, shift the whole trip forward three or four days — Fuji doesn't care about leaves.
